# Data Purpose Boundary Design

## Status

Approved for implementation on 2026-09-06.

## Purpose

JPV-OS must prevent personal data collected, received, inferred, resolved, or enriched for one legitimate purpose from silently becoming usable for a materially different purpose merely because a vendor, platform, institution, government actor, data broker, analytics provider, identity service, or downstream partner can technically connect the records.

This design converts the existing People Protection privacy, consent, data-minimization, anti-surveillance, and institutional-misuse principles into enforceable data-lineage and purpose-boundary requirements.

The governing rule is simple: technical linkability does not create permission.

## Scope

This applies across:

- JayPVentures LLC enterprise infrastructure
- jaypventures creator and public-facing systems
- jaypVLabs research and validation systems
- JPV-OS governance, entitlement, identity, routing, and orchestration layers
- all vendors, subprocessors, contractors, partners, APIs, data brokers, analytics products, identity products, fraud products, enrichment products, government-facing integrations, and future acquisitions or replacements

The rule applies whether data is collected directly from a person, obtained from a vendor, inferred from other records, purchased, licensed, matched, resolved, enriched, received through a legal request, or generated by automated systems.

## Problem Statement

The current repository establishes strong human-protection principles, including informed consent, privacy and data minimization, restrictions on cross-context linkage, prohibitions on unlawful surveillance, and review of institutional misuse. However, the current enforcement script mainly verifies the presence of required doctrine and policy artifacts. It does not currently require a machine-readable declaration of data origin, purpose, downstream recipients, cross-domain transformations, or secondary-use authorization.

This creates a structural gap: a system can satisfy textual policy checks while still lacking evidence that personal data remains within its authorized purpose boundary.

## Design Goals

The implementation must:

1. make purpose limitation enforceable rather than aspirational;
2. make data lineage explicit from source through downstream use;
3. treat secondary use and cross-domain enrichment as deny-by-default;
4. preserve legitimate, narrowly scoped processing when demonstrably authorized;
5. distinguish lawful compelled disclosure from permission for broader reuse;
6. evaluate vendors by actual capabilities and data flows, not product branding;
7. apply heightened review to sensitive and high-impact data domains;
8. fail production review when required declarations are missing, ambiguous, weakened, or internally inconsistent;
9. preserve correction, deletion, appeal, and audit evidence where applicable;
10. remain vendor-agnostic so future vendor changes cannot bypass the standard.

## Non-Goals

This design does not:

- assert that any specific healthcare dataset was transferred to an immigration, law-enforcement, intelligence, or surveillance system without evidence;
- prohibit all identity verification, fraud prevention, security analytics, healthcare administration, lawful investigations, or legally compelled disclosures;
- create duplicate privacy architecture outside the existing JPV-OS governance stack;
- rely on vendor promises as a substitute for technical and contractual evidence.

## Core Invariant

Every personal-data flow must declare and preserve a bounded purpose.

A dataset or derived record may not be used for a materially different purpose unless that new use has a documented legal and policy basis, satisfies JPV-OS People Protection and Security requirements, is within the minimum necessary scope, preserves required user rights, and passes the applicable review gate.

Cross-domain reuse is denied by default.

## Data Domains

The following domains are classified as heightened-risk:

- healthcare, claims, diagnosis, treatment, pharmacy, eligibility, benefits, payer, provider, and care-management data;
- identity, identity-proofing, identity-resolution, persistent identifiers, credentialing, biometrics, and relationship graphs;
- financial, credit, banking, payment, debt, asset, and income data;
- employment, workforce, payroll, applicant, performance, and labor data;
- precise or persistent location and movement data;
- communications metadata and contact graphs;
- educational and student data;
- child or minor data;
- civic, voter, political-participation, immigration, law-enforcement, watchlist, and government-investigative data;
- behavioral profiles, risk scores, fraud scores, predictive scores, and inferred attributes;
- any data combination that materially increases identifiability, surveillance capability, exclusion risk, or adverse-decision capability.

## Prohibited Cross-Domain Uses

Unless a narrow legal obligation requires a specific disclosure, data collected or processed for one heightened-risk domain may not silently become input to another materially unrelated heightened-risk domain.

Examples of prohibited default transitions include:

- healthcare administration -> immigration enforcement;
- healthcare administration -> unrelated law-enforcement intelligence;
- healthcare administration -> political or civic profiling;
- healthcare administration -> advertising or generalized commercial enrichment;
- identity proofing -> unrelated behavioral surveillance;
- location data -> generalized person-tracking outside the stated service purpose;
- consumer enrichment -> adverse employment, housing, insurance, immigration, civic, or public-benefit decisions without an independently valid basis and review;
- fraud prevention -> generalized watchlisting or unrelated enforcement;
- government compelled disclosure -> unrestricted downstream commercial or institutional reuse.

## Lawful Compelled Disclosure

A subpoena, court order, warrant, statute, regulatory mandate, or other lawful compulsory process may require disclosure. That does not dissolve the purpose boundary.

Any compelled disclosure must record:

- requesting authority;
- legal basis;
- date received;
- scope requested;
- scope actually disclosed;
- minimization applied;
- authorizing reviewer;
- recipient;
- retention or preservation requirement if known;
- whether notice is permitted, prohibited, delayed, or not applicable;
- whether the disclosure authorizes any subsequent use beyond the legally required purpose.

The default answer to the final item is no unless the governing law or valid authorization clearly establishes otherwise.

## Vendor Capability Review

JPV-OS must evaluate a vendor by what the integration can actually do, including:

- source datasets accessible to the vendor;
- persistent identifiers used;
- matching and identity-resolution capability;
- enrichment sources;
- derived attributes and scoring;
- relationship and household graphs;
- biometric capabilities;
- search and investigation capabilities;
- APIs and export paths;
- downstream subprocessors and partners;
- government, law-enforcement, intelligence, healthcare, financial, advertising, employment, and data-broker business lines that may create cross-domain exposure;
- contractual purpose limitations;
- deletion and correction mechanisms;
- customer-configurable sharing or retention settings;
- technical isolation between products when represented as separate systems.

A marketing claim that products are separate is not sufficient evidence of data separation.

## Machine-Readable Data Flow Declaration

Systems that process personal data must include a declaration under `governance/data-flows/`.

The initial schema will use JSON for deterministic validation.

Each declaration must include:

- `system_id`
- `owner`
- `surface`
- `data_classes`
- `source`
- `original_purpose`
- `allowed_purposes`
- `prohibited_purposes`
- `legal_basis`
- `vendors`
- `recipients`
- `persistent_identifiers`
- `identity_resolution`
- `enrichment`
- `retention`
- `deletion_correction_path`
- `cross_domain_transfer`
- `compelled_disclosure_path`
- `audit_log`
- `reviewed_by`
- `review_date`

For any cross-domain transfer, the declaration must additionally specify:

- source domain;
- destination domain;
- exact fields transferred;
- justification;
- minimization;
- authorization;
- safeguards;
- expiration or review date.

Missing fields fail validation.

## Enforcement Model

A new validator will parse all declarations and fail when:

- required fields are absent;
- allowed and prohibited purposes conflict;
- a heightened-risk domain is present without an explicit review path;
- `identity_resolution` or `enrichment` is enabled without declared purpose and safeguards;
- a cross-domain transfer is declared without justification, authorization, minimization, and review date;
- a system has personal data but no data-flow declaration;
- a vendor is marked high-impact but no capability review exists;
- compelled disclosure is treated as blanket downstream reuse permission;
- retention is undefined;
- deletion or correction is unavailable without an explicit legal or operational justification;
- an audit-log location is absent;
- policy files omit the invariant or weaken deny-by-default language.

## High-Impact Vendor Registry

A machine-readable registry under `governance/vendors/` will classify vendors by capabilities rather than reputation.

A vendor enters heightened review when it provides one or more of:

- identity resolution;
- cross-dataset enrichment;
- biometric matching;
- persistent person identifiers;
- fraud/risk scoring;
- relationship graphs;
- government investigative search;
- healthcare data aggregation;
- large-scale consumer data brokerage;
- precise location aggregation;
- adverse-decision support;
- broad downstream API access.

The registry is not a blacklist. It is a mandatory review trigger.

The design must support providers such as LexisNexis Risk Solutions, Palantir, Experian, TransUnion, Equifax, ID-verification vendors, healthcare data aggregators, and future equivalents without hard-coding the policy around any one company.

## Healthcare Boundary

Healthcare data receives explicit heightened protection because claims, eligibility, provider, pharmacy, diagnosis, treatment, utilization, care-management, and member identity data can become unusually powerful when joined with commercial identity-resolution and public-record systems.

JPV-OS therefore requires:

- healthcare-purpose declarations to enumerate every non-care and non-administrative downstream use;
- healthcare data to be blocked from unrelated policing, immigration, political, advertising, employment, generalized consumer enrichment, or watchlist uses by default;
- healthcare vendors with identity-resolution, public-record, consumer-data, or government-investigative capabilities to receive high-impact vendor review;
- any lawful compelled disclosure to remain scoped to the legal demand and independently logged.

## Product and Acquisition Drift

Purpose boundaries survive:

- vendor acquisitions;
- mergers;
- product renames;
- new APIs;
- new subprocessors;
- executive changes;
- government contract expansion;
- new customer segments;
- platform consolidation.

A material vendor capability change triggers re-review before continued production use when the change could alter data linkage, recipient access, secondary use, government access, enrichment, scoring, or retention.

## Testing

Testing must include at minimum:

1. valid single-purpose flow passes;
2. missing purpose fails;
3. missing retention fails;
4. undeclared identity resolution fails;
5. undeclared enrichment fails;
6. healthcare -> unrelated law-enforcement flow fails by default;
7. healthcare -> immigration flow fails by default;
8. identity proofing -> behavioral surveillance fails by default;
9. properly documented narrow cross-domain exception passes only with full authorization metadata;
10. compelled disclosure without scope metadata fails;
11. compelled disclosure claiming blanket downstream reuse fails;
12. high-impact vendor without capability review fails;
13. vendor acquisition/capability-change marker requires re-review;
14. deletion/correction omission without justification fails;
15. policy weakening removes required language and fails repository enforcement.

## Error Handling

Validation errors must be deterministic and actionable. Each failure must identify:

- file path;
- rule violated;
- relevant field;
- required remediation.

The validator must not silently infer missing authorization or purposes.

## Auditability

The final system must make it possible to answer, for any governed flow:

- where did this data come from;
- why was it collected;
- who can use it;
- who actually received it;
- what transformations or enrichment occurred;
- whether identifiers were resolved or linked;
- whether the purpose changed;
- who authorized any exception;
- when the exception expires or must be reviewed;
- how the person can correct or delete data where applicable;
- what evidence proves the rules were followed.

## Production Gate

A personal-data system is not production-ready unless its purpose boundary is declared, validated, reviewable, and consistent with People Protection, Security, and Governance.

No vendor, institution, government actor, customer, partner, administrator, acquisition, product rename, API expansion, or operational convenience may convert technical access into permission for an undeclared secondary use.

## Success Criteria

The change is complete only when:

- the new doctrine exists and is binding;
- machine-readable data-flow and vendor review schemas exist;
- deterministic validation is implemented;
- positive and negative regression tests pass;
- existing policy enforcement requires the new artifacts;
- People Protection, Security, Governance, production review, policy index, enforcement map, and ownership routing reference the invariant;
- a PR exposes the complete diff for independent review;
- verification demonstrates that unauthorized secondary use, undeclared cross-domain propagation, and silent vendor capability expansion are blocked at the repository governance gate.
